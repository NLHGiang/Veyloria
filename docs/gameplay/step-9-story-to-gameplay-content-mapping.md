# STEP 9 — STORY → GAMEPLAY CONTENT MAPPING

## Purpose

Step 9 translates Veyloria's established story and gameplay foundations into a concrete playable content architecture.

Step 8 defined:

> **HOW Veyloria should be played.**

Step 9 defines:

> **HOW Veyloria's story becomes playable content.**

The core mapping is:

> **Story → Quest → Activity → Mechanic → Level → Map → Progression → World State → Memory → Consequence**

## Objectives

Step 9 must answer:

- How does a story beat become a playable situation?
- How does a situation become a quest?
- How does a quest determine player actions?
- Which mechanics support those actions?
- How does the level support the intended behavior?
- How does progression emerge from understanding?
- How does the world remember player actions?
- How do consequences persist?
- How does gameplay create the next story question?

## Core Translation Pipeline

> **Story Intent → Story Beat → Player Situation → Player Question → Player Action → Gameplay Activity → Mechanic → Level / Space → World Response → Consequence → New Information → New Question**

## Core Principle

> **Turn narrative information into player experience.**

Story establishes intent, characters, canonical truth, relationships, emotional context, contradictions and major events.

Gameplay establishes what the player does, notices, investigates, chooses, changes, misunderstands and discovers.

> **Story defines meaning and circumstances. Gameplay defines player action and discovery.**

## Content Layers

Every important content unit should be understood across ten layers:

1. Story
2. Question
3. Situation
4. Action
5. Activity
6. Mechanic
7. Space
8. World Response
9. Consequence
10. Understanding

## Story Beat Rule

Every important story beat should produce at least one of:

- player action;
- player observation;
- player investigation;
- player choice;
- world-state change;
- relationship change;
- evidence;
- contradiction;
- consequence;
- reinterpretation.

A beat that produces none of these is supporting narrative rather than core gameplay.

## Quest Principle

> **Quest ≠ Objective List**

A quest is a container for a question, situation, possible actions, evidence, world response, consequence and progression.

Objective clarity should coexist with narrative uncertainty:

> **Clarity of action + Ambiguity of meaning**

## Activity Model

Core activities derive from the five gameplay pillars:

### Explore
Movement, discovery, navigation and revisit.

### Notice & Investigate
Observation, inspection, questioning, evidence comparison and contradiction.

### Interact & Change
Objects, spaces and contextual actions that alter the world.

### Memory & Contradiction
Recognition, conflicting memories and comparison between past and present.

### Consequence & World Remembers
NPC reactions, changed locations, relationships, access and persistent effects.

## Mechanic Selection Rule

> **Theme / Player Need → Gameplay Problem → Player Action → Activity → Mechanic → Feedback → Consequence**

Never design a mechanic first and then search for a reason to use it.

## Level & Map Principle

A level should create opportunities for observation, discovery, investigation, interaction, uncertainty, evidence, environmental storytelling and revisit.

A useful Veyloria level answers:

> **What can the player notice here that they could not notice elsewhere?**

Maps should communicate relationships, state, history, access, memory and consequence—not merely geography.

## Progression Principle

> **Understanding > Power**

Content may unlock knowledge, interpretation, dialogue, relationships, access, interactions, evidence or new possibilities.

> **Knowing more about Veyloria is itself progression.**

## World State & Memory

Important content should identify effects on:

- object state;
- location state;
- NPC state;
- systemic world state.

Memory is not a collectible resource.

> **The world remembers what the player did.**

Memory manifests through behavior, dialogue, relationships, objects, locations, evidence, contradictions and future consequences.

## Consequence Principle

### Minor
Local dialogue, object or NPC reaction.

### Significant
Relationship, quest, location or opportunity change.

### Major
Act progression, persistent world state, major relationship shift or large recontextualization.

> **Not every action needs a branch. Every meaningful action needs a trace.**

## Revisit Principle

> **Old Place → New Evidence → Changed Context → New Meaning**

Revisit is a content mechanism for recontextualization, not merely content reuse.

## Evidence & Contradiction

Evidence should encourage:

> **Notice → Think → Connect → Act**

Contradictions may exist between:

- memory and reality;
- NPCs;
- NPC and environment;
- past and present;
- player interpretation and later evidence;
- character belief and canonical truth.

Contradiction should create a reason to investigate.

## Narrative Footprint

Every major story event should have at least one gameplay footprint.

Every significant gameplay action should have potential to create:

- memory;
- state;
- relationship;
- evidence;
- consequence;
- future interpretation.

> **Story and gameplay must leave traces in each other.**

## Player Knowledge Model

Keep distinct:

1. **Canonical Truth**
2. **Character Memory**
3. **Player Memory**
4. **Player Interpretation**

The gap between these layers is a primary source of curiosity.

## Content Quality Test

Before production, ask:

1. What is the story intent?
2. What should the player notice?
3. What question is created?
4. What can the player actually do?
5. Which activity supports it?
6. Which mechanic enables it?
7. Why is that mechanic necessary?
8. What does the level contribute?
9. How does the world respond?
10. What changes afterward?
11. What does the player learn?
12. Is there contradiction or reinterpretation?
13. Can the player revisit this information?
14. Does the action leave a trace?
15. Does the content reinforce Veyloria's identity?

## Completion Criterion

Step 9 is complete when any major story beat can be traced through:

> **Story Intent → Story Beat → Player Question → Situation → Player Action → Activity → Mechanic → Level → World Response → Consequence → Memory → New Understanding → New Question**

At that point, the story is no longer only written.

> **It is playable.**

## North Star

> **Do not turn the story into a list of tasks.**

Turn the story into situations that the player can investigate, act upon, change and remember.

> **Story creates the question.**
>
> **Gameplay lets the player pursue it.**
>
> **The world responds.**
>
> **The consequence persists.**
>
> **New evidence changes interpretation.**
>
> **The player understands more than before.**
